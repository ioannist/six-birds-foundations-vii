# G1-L4 Prediction - Aliquot Ledger Illustration

## Hypothesis
For each starting value `1 <= n <= 10^5`, the aliquot sequence
`s(n)=sigma(n)-n` can be tracked exactly for at most `10^3` iterations, stopping
if a value exceeds `10^12` and recording that case as `escaped_cap`. Most tested
starts should terminate at `0` or enter a detected cycle, including fixed
points from perfect numbers and known amicable pairs. A minority may escape the
cap or remain unresolved at the step budget; that is expected for this
illustrative open instance, not a correctness failure.

## Predicted Outcome
All arithmetic is exact. Each `sigma(n)` is computed from an exact prime
factorization, with spot checks against direct divisor summation on small
values. The run records terminated, cycled, escaped-cap, and step-cap counts,
plus a factorization-supply ledger projection `nu_2(sigma(n_k))` along traced
orbits.

## Falsification Condition
Any exact-arithmetic inconsistency falsifies the exhibit's implementation:
for example, a factorized `sigma(n)` disagreeing with a direct brute-force
divisor sum on checked small values, a detected cycle that does not actually
repeat under `s`, or a non-exact transition in a recorded aliquot orbit. An
escaped-cap or step-cap case is not a falsification; it is part of the open
aliquot boundedness picture.

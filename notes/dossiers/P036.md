## Paper thesis

Near-minimal cosmological bounds on the summed neutrino mass can shift under seemingly benign pipeline/likelihood updates, yet standard presentations rarely include lens-level stability diagnostics that distinguish cosmology from packaging effects. We address the inconsistency highlighted by , where swapping the SPT-3G 2018 TT/TE/EE likelihood for the later SPT-3G D1 release—analyzing essentially the same sky field—tightens constraints and can move posteriors toward the physical boundary =0 . Our approach is to cast this lens swap as a packaging stability problem in the Six Birds / SBT formalism . We introduce a directional cross-lens audit functional, operationalize it via a profiled/localized/common-staging workflow, and demonstrate the resulting empirical stability picture under matched controls. Concretely, we (i) compute directional held-out mismatch statistics _ B|A and _ A|B , (ii) localize mismatch by spectrum and multipole, (iii) repeat under a common-staging multipole-support control, and (iv) reproduce the tightening under DESI DR2 BAO for two baselines (SPT-only+DESI; Planck-subset+PR4-lensing CMB baseline+DESI). We find that cross-lens mismatch is large and asymmetric; profiling reduces but does not remove the failure; common-staging changes the profiled mismatch only at the few-percent level; and localization shows TT high- structure (roughly 2000 – 3000 ) dominates within the overlap region. Under fixed BAO, swapping SPT2018 D1 tightens the 95% upper bound by O (10^ -1 ,eV) in the SPT-only+DESI regime and by O (10^ -2 ,eV) in the Planck-subset baseline+DESI regime. These artifacts turn an ambiguous parameter shift into a reproducible, localized diagnosis of lens disagreement and provide a disciplined contract for follow-up tests (including raw-data reprocessing diagnostics already emphasized by ). We are explicit that identifying the pipeline root cause or establishing a definitive value is not done here; our conclusions rely on audits of released likelihood objects under matched controls and the physical gate 0 .

## Paper thesis

studies strict-test-to-event-package admissibility on fixed support in Six Birds Theory. The starting point is the substrate: accepted new-layer strict tests on support, with the strict-test partition rung valid by inheritance. The paper asks whether this strict-test substrate can be advanced to event-package construction. The method combines identity-lane discipline, a comparison-base attempt, the inherited event-bridge record discipline, and the event-gate threshold and control stages. These steps separate scoped identity evidence from event-level package evidence. The paper obtains two paper-10-new results. First, same-role cross-context edges are accepted at scope, forming a protocol-indexed shared-identity layer. Second, the is identified as the named missing subobject required for event-gate threshold construction; this is a constructive non-definability finding, not a refutation of future construction. The final verdict is the series-canonical : the event-level rung requires further extension, and the verdict is earned by attempting the admissibility chain to the named obstruction. The claim boundary is explicit. Shared-event identity in the strong sense is not claimed; no event-package candidate or proto-effect structures are constructed; and inherited nonclaims from and are preserved. A Lean structural kernel with modules and proof gaps records the formal companion discipline.

## Paper thesis

Certifying multiscale anti-localization only at t=0 is not useful unless legality is forward-closed; we lack general, mechanizable conditions under which SPT-legality propagates under time evolution. We propose a slot-audited channelized-feasibility contract in a Hilbert/band/cell model, where legality is defined by the normalized max-cell concentration audit _j and enforced structurally by feasible representations F _j=Ran( _j) with packaging projectors E_j . We introduce these objects and theorem-grade constants (delocalization, frame bounds, mode growth, and cellwise defect), operationalize them with standardized experiment outputs and an automated audit.json, and evaluate the resulting bounds in toy dynamics together with matched failure-mode controls. In an invariant-ladder regime (exact feasibility invariance), _j(t) stays uniformly below the predicted envelope K^ pred _ glob ; injecting a globally tiny but cell-localized off-feasible component causes immediate deep-band violations despite leakage below 10^ -2 . With per-channel delocalization held fixed, worsening frame conditioning produces extreme tails, whereas well-conditioned artifacts keep far below the envelope; under cellwise defect control def^ _ f_j , maxima grow monotonically with _ and remain within the robust bound K^ rob ( _ ) . This yields a closed, regression-tested regime for ``SPT-legal evolution'' in the model and pinpoints which structural slots are necessary (and which global diagnostics are misleading) when building auditable multiscale representations. We are explicit that stronger claims—PDE smoothing, physical entropy production, or emergent learning of channel maps—are not established here; our conclusions hold in a finite-dimensional Hilbert/cell setting and rely on theorem-aligned bounds together with controlled counterexamples.

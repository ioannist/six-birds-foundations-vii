## Paper thesis

Previous demonstrations of contextuality-type obstruction start from quantum mechanics or from externally specified measurement scenarios. We show that the same locally-Boolean-but-globally-obstructed structure can arise from an autonomous emergence framework—Six Birds Theory—without importing Hilbert space, amplitudes, or the Born rule. The formal vehicle is a finite event package: a family of stable-record contexts, each carrying a finite Boolean event algebra, together with a layer-relative shared-event identification and an audit layer. Obstruction is the failure of admissible global realization for that package. The computational framework combines exact and quotient-backed feasibility, structural and statistical deficits, provenance auditing, and false-positive controls. The evidence base includes a classical control, a boundary-case epistemic calibration, an explicit finite parity/context witness, and autonomous discovery organized by mechanism, lens, and packaging axes. The results are stratified: mechanism variation yields package-sensitive structure with a quotient-backed witness under a weaker claim ceiling; lens variation closes with a cross-resolution strict extension obstruction on fixed support; and packaging variation produces the strongest current obstruction, subject to a package conflict caveat. Where applicable, the principal cases persist under bounded robustness checks. The paper therefore provides the first computationally explicit evidence that contextuality-type obstruction can emerge from a system's own packaging structure rather than from quantum theory or an externally imposed scenario.

## Paper thesis

Accounting-style inequalities are central in Six Birds Theory, but they do not by themselves rule out catastrophic extremes: highly localized ``needle'' probes can satisfy global budgets while producing large peaks. We ask when packaging plus accounting forces anti-localization, and propose Reflexive SBT: a capacity-based audit of the packaged artifacts (subspaces, feasibility dictionaries, and route ladders). We define a localization metric _j at cell scale 2^ -j , compute it via an eigenvalue method, and upper-bound it using three auditable capacities: subspace trace/diagonal dispersion, sparse feasibility conditioning on small supports, and a route-union ledger charging multi-route inflation. We find three controlled separations: (i) shift-invariant channels (Fourier/circulant) have uniform projector diagonals and _j follows the deterministic m/2^j bound, while diagonal disorder drives _j rapidly toward 1 compared with full-noise perturbations; (ii) feasibility-level needles arise exactly when small-support conditioning collapses ( sigma_ → 0 ) and are absent in random baselines; (iii) for spectral packaging, route mismatch spikes only at degenerate cutoffs and is eliminated by a canonical tie-break, with remaining route effects captured by the union ledger. This turns the anti-localization hinge into a reproducible checklist and yields a conditional theorem: vanishing capacity implies _j→ 0 along a ladder. We are explicit that Reflexive SBT is not proven here to universally drive capacities to zero; we prove the capacity implies anti-localization implications and provide auditable closure evidence on canonical failure and repair modes.

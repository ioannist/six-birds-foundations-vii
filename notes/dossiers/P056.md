## Paper thesis

We propose that to be real is to have closed: that reality is not a substance, a substrate, or a set, but a ledger of survived closures. A thing is real when it is a package that earned its objecthood through audited gates and pays, continuously, to keep it. The proposal is grounded in the Six Birds corpus, which has spent the last years doing something Nietzsche predicted and no one else has attempted: worshipping the stone. The corpus constructs geometry, logic, time, probability, objects, events, agents, institutions, and selves from bare substrates, under explicit audit, with every failure banked and every success priced. The method of this paper is an itemized metaphysics. Every claim is either free, a theorem of the corpus at its stated grade, or purchased, spending one of two named axioms: Ontic Closure Completeness and Substrate Indifference. On this basis we argue that the Born rule's shape is the generic fate of compressed record layers, so that probability is born at the lens; that a simulation which passes a closure's gates is not a simulation of the thing but an instance of it, which is why lattice QCD computes the true proton; that ``matter'' and ``information'' are altitude labels, so that an economy is literally a ghost made of people; that mathematics is closure engineering in vitro, the design of lawful theory extensions ahead of any run, and in practice a map that unfolds only where someone walks; that the explanatory gap of consciousness is a non-descent certificate, predicted rather than embarrassing; that life and intelligence are graded, auditable positions on real axes rather than binaries; that a civilization which learns this grammar crosses into the era of meta-emergence, where creation itself is a wielded power, and that peace is a closure state, an achievement that strengthens rather than dissolves the identities it comprises; and that the great paradoxes (sorites, Theseus, Zeno, Mary's room, the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics, the arrow of time) dissolve under ledger discipline, most of them because they demanded of a layer a certificate that no layer can issue. We close eight old problems and open eight new ones. The last sacrifice is Sacrifice itself.

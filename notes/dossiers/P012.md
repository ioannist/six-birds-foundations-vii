## Paper thesis

We introduce a fixed-support route-transport framework in which histories can be equivalent at a current event interface yet differ as predictors of later admissible observations. For each interface, we define a current quotient by present-event indistinguishability and a predictive quotient by equality of all later admissible event statistics under continuation. Standard quotient-algebraic results then apply in this setting: future-predictive equivalence refines current-event equivalence, admissible continuations induce well-defined transport on , and the current class after transport is determined by the source predictive class through a canonical mixed map _i _j . Generic transport on along all outgoing continuations exists precisely at flat interfaces, where current equivalence already determines all future observations. The predictive quotient is future-sufficient, and every future-sufficient state abstraction factors uniquely through on the reachable image. Strict refinement of is equivalent to existence of a pair of histories that are current-equivalent but future-distinct. For endo-continuations, representative push can preserve the current class of every history while moving a predictive class. The contribution of the paper is the framework itself and the demonstration that it is non-vacuous: an axiom-audited finite memory-wheel instance in Lean and exact finite fixed-support benchmarks instantiate strict refinement and the loop asymmetry, while the computational suite separates artifact, flattenable, explicit-latent, dissipative, and coherent-candidate regimes with bounded robustness under perturbation. Small discovery searches recover additional coherent candidates beyond the hand-built examples while a control search space remains all-flat.

## Paper thesis

This paper proves a conditional separation between P and NP inside Six Birds Theory, a typed framework for closure formation in mathematical structures. For SAT, the framework supplies a saturated SAT layer — a typed carrier of polynomial-time SAT computations together with their audit data — observed through a lawful polynomial-time instrument that declares which observations on the layer count as current. Two translation theorems connect this setup to standard SAT semantics. Translation A identifies SAT P with decision currentization: the SAT decision bit is itself a current observation on . Translation B identifies decision currentization with currentization of the canonical witness producer of the autonomous lift , using SAT self-reducibility and closure of current observables under post-processing, finite products, and composition. The structural hypothesis of says that this canonical witness producer is not a lawful current observable on . Under that hypothesis, the four-step contradiction chain gives SAT P ; combined with SAT NP from the standard verifier package, the framework accepts at recognition grade. Outside the framework, the result is the conditional theorem: formed-SAT closure carrying under implies P NP . The same closure records also tag , in the audit-closure register of , as a carrier-attached cascade-stable AOR package under . This is packaging of the same conditional theorem. The hypothesis further localizes to one residual: the canonical lexicographic SAT-branching readout is not a lawful current observable on the standard fixed-presentation polynomial current carrier. A scoped Class-B Irremovability theorem rules out one removal route: no basis-generated Class-B carrier-feature method can remove that residual, except by leaving the basis or importing a target-equivalent lower bound. The paper does not claim an unconditional standard-ZFC separation of P and NP , derivability of from framework primitives, circumvention of the classical barriers in their native senses, absolute irremovability of , or any AOR claim outside the stated formed-SAT, standard-basis scope.

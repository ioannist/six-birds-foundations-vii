## Problem and thesis

Repeated interaction routinely produces structure absent from the one-shot game: reputations, norms, conventions, and other institutions. The problem is to say what this structure is, when the bare game predicts play, and where the extra structure comes from. This paper argues that the institutional content of repeated play is a strict extension of the stage game: a refinement with genuinely new predictive distinctions not definable as a function of the game's own vocabulary.

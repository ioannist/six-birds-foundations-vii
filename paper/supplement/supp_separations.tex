\subsection{Statements that follow from the definitions}
\label{supp:definitional}

The catalog entries below are definitions, or statements that follow in a line from the
definition of the certificate they concern (main paper, \Cref{sec:sep-definitional}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Neutral seeds.} A seed certified as neutral authorizes a first extension,
        but so does any admitted seed: authorization uses membership in the list of seeds,
        not the neutrality certificate. Two refusals attach to neutrality: a seed stocked
        after the fact earns no neutral credit, and a system-generated source routed
        through an external label is not thereby external.
  \item \emph{Endogenous enablement.} The endogeny criterion is the definition of endogeny
        (\Cref{def:enablement}) read as an equivalence. Not definitional is that the two
        hidden-executor clauses are independent of the others: the reference world
        contains a theorist-triggered and an observer-triggered profile, each satisfying
        every other condition.
  \item \emph{Downward selection.} A transmission record is a \emph{pure downward
        selection} when it inserts nothing from outside and every lower-layer fact present
        after the selection was present before it or was inserted. With insertion
        excluded, no lower-layer fact is created: a downward explanation may not populate
        the lower layer by describing it. \Cref{prop:selection} proves this for selection
        modelled as intersection.
  \item \emph{Horizons.} Non-occurrence within a finite horizon licenses only
        horizon-qualified conclusions unless the search family is closed, the detector's
        power is established and the null is complete beyond the horizon. These are fields
        of the negative-evidence record, so the statement fixes what that record requires;
        \Cref{prop:search} proves the corresponding implications for an explicit search.
  \item \emph{Certified non-interaction.} A claim that two theories do not interact is
        admitted only with its contact channels, detector power, budget, horizon and family
        closure declared, and it covers only the declared family.
  \item \emph{Composition of enablement.} The resource deltas of enablement links (cost
        and residual debt) combine associatively with a two-sided identity, for all
        inputs. The composability conditions (matching source types, execution witnesses,
        budgets and residual obligations) are not needed for associativity; they govern
        whether a composed chain earns credit. A two-link chain whose result depends on the
        order of its links is exhibited.
  \item \emph{Confluence at a declared scope.} A criterion on a list of critical-pair
        records (every legal pair is marked joinable and audit-equivalent) is equivalent to
        confluence at the declared scope. Both sides are predicates on the same records,
        so the equivalence is a reformulation and proves no path confluence; the semantic
        statement is \Cref{thm:admission}.
  \item \emph{Join status and the strict-join certificate.} Over each declared interface
        family the classifier assigns exactly one of the seven statuses of
        \Cref{def:join-status}, as checked by exhaustive enumeration of the declared
        carriers. A valid strict-join certificate has objecthood, retention of both
        parents and a valid anti-product witness, and dropping any one of these admits a
        configuration that the certificate must refuse. These statements project fields of
        the certificate; their semantic counterpart is \Cref{thm:bridge}.
  \item \emph{Order residue.} A route comparison has residue by \Cref{def:route}. The
        reference world exhibits residue without a drive certificate, which is why residue
        does not supply an arrow.
  \item \emph{The join-created residual.} The reference world contains a ledger
        transition that discharges one inherited residual and records one new residual
        marked as created at the composite interface. The mark is recorded, not derived
        from a model of the join: the ledger can represent this situation and must keep
        the two kinds apart.
  \item \emph{Cross-family corollaries.} The catalog's 21 corollaries are conjunctions and
        projections of these certificates across clusters: a certified configuration
        carries several certificates at once, and each is discharged on its own. They are
        listed in \Cref{supp:register}.
\end{itemize}

When may two theories interact lawfully, and what must a joint theory show to count as
more than its parts? This paper gives a typed certificate language for such claims and
proves where mathematics backs its certificates. The language keeps apart claims
that are easily conflated: that content is expressible, present, reachable or occurrent;
that two theories touch or form a joint theory; that one enables another or determines
it; that order matters or time has an arrow. Twenty-seven named
countermodels in a finite reference world exhibit these separations. An observable of a join is strict, factoring on its image through
neither parent nor their pairing, exactly when it has a split pair against each of the three maps. When admitting an item can only
enable further admissions, every admission order reaches the same terminal state, by
Newman's lemma, and one disabling guard produces two. As a soundness check, a semantic strict join
satisfies the language's strictness certificate once it is also proved not to factor, on
its image, through a declared refinement and schedule, which strictness alone does not
give. Other entries are definitions or recorded refusals. The results are formalized in Lean~4, with
the exceptions stated in \Cref{sec:lean}.
